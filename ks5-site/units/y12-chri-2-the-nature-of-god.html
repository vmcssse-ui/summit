The Nature of God

One unit, one question — asked seven ways. What do Christians mean when they call God one, omnipotent, triune, personal — and does every definition hold?

The
big
question

What is the nature of the Christian God — and how far can human language and doctrine capture it?

02

The same question, seven ways

The unit splits into seven sub-topics. They look different, but each is really asking what is the nature of God, and can we capture it?

Sub-topic 1

Christian Monotheism

What does one God mean?
EthicalOne God = one moral authority (covenant).
PropheticIsaiah: only one God exists at all.
DevelopmentIsrael moved from many gods to one.
Shema“The Lord our God, the Lord is one.”
Sub-topic 2

Creator, Omnipotent, Benevolent

Can the attributes hold?
OmnipotentAll-powerful Creator ex nihilo.
BenevolentAll-loving — the inconsistent triad.
TheodicyAugustine / Hick keep all three.
ProcessDrop omnipotence to solve evil.
Sub-topic 3

The Importance of the Trinity

Is God one or three?
CoherenceHolds sin, atonement, redemption.
PersonalTranscendent AND immanent.
PersonhoodGod is love; a model for us.
OrthodoxOne substance, three persons.
Sub-topic 4

Jesus as the Son of God

Is Jesus God or man?
TrinitarianLiterally God the Son (John 10:30).
PurposeOne in will, not essence (hen).
LiberalAn exceptional, godly man.
Kurios1 Cor 8:6 — Lord, but ambiguous.
Sub-topic 5

Personal Language

Does human language fit?
PersonalGod answers prayer — Abba.
Father / KingStrength, authority, love.
ChallengeDoes it limit God (Freud)?
AnalogicalTrue of God, not literal.
Sub-topic 6

Feminist Critique

Is God-talk too male?
CritiqueMale/monarchical — harms women & nature.
McFagueMother, Lover, Friend.
PanentheismThe world as ‘the body of God’.
Re-writeSupplement, don’t abandon.
Sub-topic 7

Process Theology

Is this still Christian?
Not ex nihiloGod orders eternal chaos (Griffin).
Not omnipotentGod persuades, not coerces.
StrengthSolves the problem of evil.
WeaknessA non-omnipotent God worth worship?

04

How to revise this unit

Use your resources in this order. The Philosopher Companion is where you learn to plan both question types.

Watch the lesson & listen to the podcast

One podcast per sub-topic — a teacher and student talk each view through to a judgement. Annotate as you listen.

Complete the greyscale booklet

Read the given text, write the key claims in your own words, then decide the strength and weakness you'd give each view.

Match & apply the key terms

In the Companion: match each term to its meaning, then use it in a sentence. RAG-rate the mastery checklist with the three faces.

Plan AO1, then build AO2 on top

Use the Companion frameworks and the guidance-left / your-turn-right scaffolds. Never evaluate what you haven't first explained.

AO1 · Examine the views (10)
What it is — the factual chain: define authority, then set out each view in order and apply the evidence (e.g. Genesis).
Aim — explain the whole range accurately and neutrally. No judging yet.
AO2 · Evaluate the claim (15)
What it is — a three-paragraph dialectic: view, then evaluation → counter-evaluation → implication; mirror it; then judge.
Aim — weigh the views, use one as a lens on another, and reach a justified judgement that links back to your thesis.
