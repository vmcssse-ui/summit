Good Conduct & Moral Principles

One unit, one argument — told three times. Because humans are made in the image of God, conduct has stakes, life is sacred, and the Earth is held in trust.

The
big
question

How must Christians live — and what may they do to a life that God has made sacred?

The thread that ties it together

Top answers don't treat the three debates separately. One text runs through all of them — spot the thread and you're writing at the top of the mark scheme.

The image of God

Genesis 1:26 grounds all three debates: conduct, sanctity and dominion all flow from what humans are made AS.

Sacred → sanctity

If the image makes life sacred, the embryo, the dying and the enemy soldier all fall under one principle — tested to breaking.

Image → answerability

An image answers to what it images. The same verse read as licence or as trusteeship is the whole dominion debate.

04

How to revise this unit

Use your resources in this order — for each of the three sub-topics. The Philosopher Companion is where you learn to plan both question types.

Watch the lesson & listen to the podcast

One podcast per sub-topic — a teacher and student talk each view through to a judgement. Annotate as you listen.

Complete the greyscale booklet

Read the given text, write the key claims in your own words, then decide the strength and weakness you'd give each view.

Match & apply the key terms

In the Companion: match each term to its meaning, then use it in a sentence. RAG-rate the mastery checklist with the three faces.

Plan AO1, then build AO2 on top

Use the Companion frameworks and the guidance-left / your-turn-right scaffolds. Never evaluate what you haven't first explained.

AO1 · Examine the views (10)
What it is — the factual chain: define the key idea, then set out each view in order and apply the evidence (e.g. Genesis 1:26).
Aim — explain the whole range accurately and neutrally. No judging yet.
AO2 · Evaluate the claim (15)
What it is — a three-paragraph dialectic: view, then evaluation → counter-evaluation → implication; mirror it; then judge.
Aim — weigh the views, use one as a lens on another, and reach a justified judgement that links back to your thesis.
