The thread that ties it together

Top answers don't treat the three lessons separately. Three ideas run through all of them — spot the thread and you're writing at the top of the mark scheme.

The ladder of being

Contingent things → caused necessary beings → ONE uncaused necessary being. Way 3 climbs it; every criticism tries to cut a rung.

When may explanation stop?

Aquinas stops at the necessary being; Russell stops at the brute fact; Hare says your blik decides. That is the whole argument in one question.

Evenly balanced

Every criticism has a reply, and every reply a rejoinder. The essay-winning move is to adjudicate — not just to list.

04

How to revise this unit

Use your resources in this order. The Philosopher Companion is where you learn to plan both question types.

Watch the lesson & listen to the podcast

One podcast per lesson — a teacher and student rebuild the argument and weigh it to a judgement. Annotate as you listen.

Complete the greyscale booklet

Read the given text, write the key claims of each step or criticism in your own words, then decide the strength and weakness you'd give it.

Match & apply the key terms

In the Companion: match each term to its meaning, then use it in a sentence. RAG-rate the mastery checklist with the three faces.

Plan AO1, then build AO2 on top

Use the Companion frameworks — including the real AQA 2020 question and the 2019/2023 dialogues. Never evaluate what you haven't first explained.

AO1 · Examine the views (10)
What it is — the factual chain: define contingency, then rebuild the argument P1→C3 — or set out each criticism WITH its reply.
Aim — explain the whole chain accurately and neutrally. No judging yet.
AO2 · Evaluate the claim (15)
What it is — a three-paragraph dialectic: position, then evaluation → counter-evaluation → implication; mirror it; then judge.
Aim — adjudicate each criticism-and-reply pair, rank them, and reach a judgement that links back to your thesis.
