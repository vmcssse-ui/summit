Normative Ethical Theories: Natural Moral Law

One unit, one argument — told three times. Can reason alone, reading a fixed human nature, deliver a moral law binding on everyone?

The
big
question

Does natural moral law survive contact with a real, particular case — or does the rule break the moment it meets one?

The thread that ties it together

Top answers don't treat the three sub-topics separately. One tension runs through all of them — spot it and you're writing at the top of the mark scheme.

Exceptionless rules vs particular cases

Every sub-topic returns to the same fault-line: can a rule that admits no exceptions survive contact with one agonising, particular case?

Reason vs revelation

Aquinas grounds the law in reason reading a common human nature — but 'worship God' and the beatific vision both quietly assume a Creator.

The proportionalist turn

Each time application gets hard — the starving man, the lying verdict, abortion — someone asks whether a PROPORTIONATE reason should override the rule.

04

How to revise this unit

Use your resources in this order. The Philosopher Companion is where you learn to plan both question types.

Watch the lesson & listen to the podcast

One podcast per sub-topic — a teacher and student talk each view through to a judgement. Annotate as you listen.

Complete the greyscale booklet

Read the given text, write the key claims in your own words, then decide the strength and weakness you'd give each view.

Match & apply the key terms

In the Companion: match each term to its meaning, then use it in a sentence. RAG-rate the mastery checklist with the three faces.

Plan AO1, then build AO2 on top

Use the Companion frameworks and the guidance-left / your-turn-right scaffolds. Never evaluate what you haven't first explained.

AO1 · Examine the theory (10)
What it is — the factual chain: define natural moral law, then set out the four-fold division, the first precept and the primary precepts in order, applying real detail (the five precepts, the telos).
Aim — explain the whole chain accurately and neutrally. No judging yet.
AO2 · Evaluate the claim (15)
What it is — a three-paragraph dialectic: view, then evaluation → counter-evaluation → implication; mirror it; then judge.
Aim — weigh the views, use one as a lens on another (e.g. proportionalism on double effect), and reach a justified judgement that links back to your thesis.
