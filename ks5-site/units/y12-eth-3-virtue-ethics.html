Aristotle's Virtue & Ethics

Every other ethical theory you study asks what should I do? This one asks something different: what sort of person should I be? Everything that follows — and every objection to it — comes from that single change of question.

The
big
question

Can an ethics built on character, rather than rules, actually tell anyone how to live?

9 questions in our past-paper bank are directly about virtue ethics — across 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024. This is not an optional topic.

The thread that ties it together

Top answers do not treat the three lessons separately. Three ideas run through all of them — spot the thread and you are writing at the top of the mark scheme.

Agent, not act

Virtue ethics asks what you should BE, not what you should DO. Every strength and every weakness follows from that one move.

Function drives everything

The Function Argument gives the goal, the goal shapes the virtues, the virtues need the mean. Break the first link and the rest wobbles.

No rules — problem or point?

Both sides agree virtue ethics issues no rules. Whether that is fatal or the whole point IS the evaluation.

04

How to revise this unit

Use your resources in this order. The Philosopher Companion is where you learn to plan all three question types.

Watch the lesson & listen to the podcast

One podcast per sub-topic — a teacher and student talk each position through to a judgement. Annotate as you listen.

Complete the greyscale booklet

Read the given text, write the key claims in your own words, then decide the strength and weakness YOU would give each position.

Match & apply the key terms

In the Companion: match each term to its meaning, then use it in a sentence of your own. RAG-rate the mastery checklist with the three faces.

Plan AO1, then build AO2 on top

Use the Companion frameworks and the guidance-left / your-turn-right scaffolds. Never evaluate what you have not first explained.

Write one timed answer from a plan only

Fifteen minutes, no notes beyond your plan. Then check it against the worked example in Part 4.

AO1 · Examine… (10 marks)
What it is — a factual chain of reasoning. Define the key term, set out the position in order, and make each link make the next necessary.
Watch for — if the question names an issue (e.g. lying), applying the theory to it is compulsory. No evaluation: there are no marks for it here.
AO2 · Evaluate this claim. (15 marks)
What it is — a three-paragraph dialectic. Thesis → strongest case for → strongest case against, weighing BOTH → the decisive consideration.
Aim — a judgement that answers the claim directly. "It has strengths and weaknesses" is not a judgement.
AO3 · Critically examine… (25 marks)
What it is — Paper 2B Section C dialogue. Virtue ethics meets Christian ethics — both traditions must be genuinely present, not just named.
