The Trinity

Year 10 starts. You get your Unit 1 booklet and your Student Companion today. Both live in your bag.

Lesson 1

The Trinity

EpisodeThe Trinity

Retrieval roulette — five questions, no notes
  1. What does “monotheism” mean?
  2. Which creed defines the Trinity?
  3. Name the three persons of the Trinity.
  4. Which event shows all three persons together?
  5. Name one non-Trinitarian group.
Score yourself out of five. Under four? This episode is tonight’s re-listen.
What this is about

Christians believe in one God in three Persons: Father, Son and Holy Spirit. Each is fully God, yet God is not three gods. The Nicene Creed sets this out. It matters because it shapes worship, baptism and prayer, and explains how God can be both beyond the world and present within it.

Key words — write your meaning in the Companion first, then check
Trinity
The Christian belief that God is three persons — Father, Son and Holy Spirit — in one God.
Monotheism
The belief that there is only one God.
Nicene Creed
Statement of Christian belief agreed by Church councils in 325 and 381, still used in worship.
Holy Spirit
The third person of the Trinity; God at work in the world, guiding and inspiring believers.
Filioque
Latin for 'and the Son'; disputed creed phrase saying the Spirit comes from Father and Son.
Source of wisdom

Matthew 28:19 — 'Go and make disciples of all nations, baptising them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.'

Practice statement'You cannot be a real Christian without believing in the Trinity.'

Agree — material

Most Catholic, Anglican and Orthodox Christians argue the Trinity is non-negotiable: the Nicene Creed makes it official Church teaching, and Jesus tells the disciples to baptise 'in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit' (Matthew 28:19).

Disagree — material

Unitarians and Jehovah's Witnesses point out that the word 'Trinity' never appears in the Bible. Jesus prays to the Father as someone separate from himself (Mark 14:36), and the Jewish Shema, which Jesus himself quotes, insists God is one (Deuteronomy 6:4).

You could nameNicene CreedCatholicsOrthodox (filioque)Unitarians
Listen for

Listen for how the speakers explain the difference between three persons and three gods, and any image they use for one God in three persons.

Lesson 2

Creation

EpisodeThe Creation of the Universe and Humanity

Retrieval roulette — five questions, no notes
  1. What does “ex nihilo” mean?
  2. Which persons of the Trinity are active in creation?
  3. What does Genesis say humans are made in?
  4. Define stewardship.
  5. Trap: does the Big Bang disprove God?
Score yourself out of five. Under four? This episode is tonight’s re-listen.
What this is about

Christians believe God made the universe from nothing, freely and out of love. Genesis 1 shows God creating by his word, with the Spirit present, and calling it good. It matters because the world belongs to God, humans are made in his image, and life has purpose rather than being an accident.

Key words — write your meaning in the Companion first, then check
Creation
God bringing the universe and everything in it into existence.
Ex nihilo
Latin for 'out of nothing'; God created without using any materials that already existed.
Genesis
The first book of the Bible, which contains the two accounts of creation.
Stewardship
The duty God gave humans to look after the earth and its creatures.
Word (Logos)
In John 1, God's creative Word, active in creation and later made flesh as Jesus.
Source of wisdom

Genesis 1:1 — 'In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth.' (John 1:1-3 adds that all things were made through the Word.)

Practice statement'Genesis should be taken literally.'

Agree — material

Young Earth creationists, like Ken Ham's Answers in Genesis, treat Genesis 1 as literal history: God made everything in six days, each with an evening and a morning. They argue scripture is God-breathed and trustworthy (2 Timothy 3:16), so denying it denies God's word.

Disagree — material

The Catholic Church and liberal Christians read Genesis as theological truth rather than science. Pope John Paul II accepted evolution as more than a hypothesis (1996), and Genesis 1 and 2 give creation in different orders, which suggests poetry rather than reporting.

You could nameliteralist Christiansliberal ChristiansBig Bang and evolutionstewardship
Listen for

Listen for how the speakers describe the role of the Word and the Spirit in creation, and any example of Christians treating Genesis as a message about why rather than how.

What this is about

Christians believe God became human in Jesus: fully God and fully man, born of the Virgin Mary. Jesus is the second Person of the Trinity taking on flesh. It matters because it shows God understands human suffering from the inside, and only a sinless God-man could die to save people from sin.

Key words — write your meaning in the Companion first, then check
Incarnation
God becoming human in Jesus; the word literally means 'made flesh'.
Emmanuel
A title meaning 'God with us', used of Jesus in Matthew 1:23.
Virgin Birth
Belief that Jesus was conceived by the Holy Spirit and born to Mary, a virgin.
Dual nature
The belief that Jesus is fully God and fully human at the same time.
Son of God
A title for Jesus showing his unique relationship with God the Father.
Source of wisdom

John 1:14 — 'The Word became flesh and made his dwelling among us.'

Practice statement'Jesus was just a human being.'

Agree — material

Non-religious thinkers, and Muslims and Jews in their own way, accept Jesus as a real teacher but not as God. The Gospels show him hungry, weeping (John 11:35) and crying out that God had forsaken him (Mark 15:34); Arius taught he was created.

Disagree — material

Mainstream Christians say Jesus has a dual nature, fully God and fully human. John's Gospel says the Word became flesh and lived among us (John 1:14); Thomas calls the risen Jesus 'my Lord and my God' (John 20:28); the Nicene Creed says 'true God from true God'.

The last days of Jesus' life

The story behind the crucifixion and resurrection. This one comes back in almost every paper.

Lesson 4

The last days of Jesus' life

EpisodeThe Last Days of Jesus

Retrieval roulette — five questions, no notes
  1. Name the five events of the final week, in order.
  2. What did Jesus institute at the Last Supper?
  3. On what day did Jesus rise?
  4. What is the ascension?
  5. Why does the resurrection matter?
Score yourself out of five. Under four? This episode is tonight’s re-listen.
What this is about

Christians remember Jesus' final week: the Last Supper, betrayal by Judas, arrest, trials before the Sanhedrin and Pilate, crucifixion, resurrection on the third day and ascension. These events matter because they show Jesus' death was a willing sacrifice for sin, and the resurrection proves death is not the end.

Key words — write your meaning in the Companion first, then check
Last Supper
Jesus' final Passover meal with the disciples, where he shared bread and wine as his body and blood.
Gethsemane
The garden where Jesus prayed in agony before he was betrayed and arrested.
Crucifixion
The Roman execution in which Jesus was nailed to a cross and died.
Resurrection
Jesus rising from the dead on the third day after his crucifixion.
Ascension
Jesus returning to heaven forty days after the resurrection, watched by his disciples.
Source of wisdom

1 Corinthians 15:3-4 — 'Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day.'

Practice statement'The resurrection is the most important Christian belief.'

Agree — material

St Paul is blunt: 'if Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile' (1 Corinthians 15:17). Christians argue the resurrection proves Jesus defeated death, guarantees believers their own resurrection, and is why Easter, not Christmas, is the Church's greatest festival.

Disagree — material

Others put the crucifixion first: the cross, not the empty tomb, is Christianity's symbol, and Anselm argued Jesus' death paid the debt of human sin. Catholic and Orthodox writers add that without the incarnation there is no saviour to raise at all.

You could name1 Corinthians 15bodily resurrectionliberal Christians (a spiritual view)the crucifixion
Listen for

Listen for what the empty tomb and the appearances actually changed for the frightened disciples — that turns the resurrection into evidence rather than a claim.

Salvation

Salvation — how Christians believe the relationship with God is repaired.

Lesson 5

Salvation

EpisodeSalvation

Retrieval roulette — five questions, no notes
  1. What is grace?
  2. What is original sin?
  3. What does atonement mean?
  4. Quote a source on salvation.
  5. Trap: do all Christians agree on how salvation works?
Score yourself out of five. Under four? This episode is tonight’s re-listen.
What this is about

Salvation means being saved from sin and its consequences, and restored to a right relationship with God. Christians believe this comes through God's grace, shown in Jesus' death and resurrection, and is received by faith. Some also stress the law and the Holy Spirit. It matters because it offers forgiveness and eternal life.

Key words — write your meaning in the Companion first, then check
Salvation
Being saved from sin and its consequences, and given eternal life with God.
Grace
God's free, undeserved love and help, given to people who have not earned it.
Redemption
Being 'bought back' from sin, which Christians believe Jesus achieved by his death.
Original sin
The flawed human nature Christians believe everyone inherits from Adam and Eve's disobedience.
Atonement
The way Jesus' death repairs the broken relationship between God and humanity.
Source of wisdom

Ephesians 2:8 — 'It is by grace you have been saved, through faith… it is the gift of God.'

Practice statement'Good deeds are enough for salvation.'

Agree — material

James says faith without works is dead (James 2:17). In Matthew 25:31-46 people are judged on whether they fed the hungry and visited prisoners. Catholic teaching, and liberation theologians like Gustavo Gutierrez, stress that real faith must show itself in action for the poor.

Disagree — material

Martin Luther and most Protestants teach justification by faith alone. Ephesians 2:8-9 says salvation is God's gift, by grace through faith and not the result of works, so nobody can boast. Original sin means humans are too flawed to earn heaven themselves.

You could nameEphesians 2:8Protestants (faith alone)Catholics (faith, works and sacraments)grace
Listen for

Listen for how the speakers link grace and works together rather than treating them as opposites — that nuance lifts an evaluation beyond a simple yes or no.

EpisodeChristian Eschatology — Beliefs About the End Times

What this is about

Eschatology is Christian belief about death and what follows. Most Christians believe in resurrection of the body, judgement by God, and heaven or hell; Catholics also believe in purgatory, a state of purification. It matters because it gives hope beyond death and a reason to live morally now.

Key words — write your meaning in the Companion first, then check
Eschatology
The study of Christian beliefs about death, judgement and the end of the world.
Judgement Day
The end of time when Christ returns and God judges every person's life.
Heaven
Eternal life in God's presence, believed to be the reward for the saved.
Hell
Eternal separation from God, believed by many to be the punishment for rejecting him.
Purgatory
Catholic belief in a state of cleansing after death before a soul enters heaven.
Source of wisdom

John 14:2-3 — 'My Father's house has many rooms… I am going there to prepare a place for you.' (Matthew 25:31-46 describes the judgement of sheep and goats.)

Agree — material

The Apostles' Creed ends with belief in 'the life everlasting', and John 3:16 promises eternal life to believers. Paul argues Christian faith is pointless if the dead are not raised (1 Corinthians 15:19). Belief in judgement also shapes how Christians live now.

Disagree — material

Liberal Christians and liberation theologians argue the Kingdom of God matters here and now: the Lord's Prayer asks for God's kingdom on earth as it is in heaven. Groups like the Salvation Army focus on poverty today, and belief in God himself is more basic.

The problem of evil and suffering

The hardest question in the course: if God is good, why is there suffering?

Lesson 7

The problem of evil and suffering

EpisodeThe Problem of Evil and Suffering

Retrieval roulette — five questions, no notes
  1. What does omnipotent mean?
  2. What does omnibenevolent mean?
  3. Give an example of moral suffering.
  4. Give an example of natural suffering.
  5. Trap: does the problem attack God’s existence or his nature?
Score yourself out of five. Under four? This episode is tonight’s re-listen.
What this is about

If God is all-loving, all-powerful and all-knowing, why does evil exist? Christians distinguish moral evil, caused by human choices, from natural evil like earthquakes and disease. The problem matters because suffering can shake faith and is used by atheists as evidence that such a God does not exist.

Key words — write your meaning in the Companion first, then check
Theodicy
An argument defending God's goodness and power despite the existence of evil.
Moral evil
Suffering caused by deliberate human choices, such as murder, war or theft.
Natural evil
Suffering caused by nature, such as earthquakes, floods or disease.
Omnipotent
All-powerful; able to do anything that is logically possible.
Omnibenevolent
All-loving; wanting only what is good for creation.
Source of wisdom

Habakkuk 1:2-3 — 'How long, Lord, must I call for help, but you do not listen?' (Psalm 22:1 makes the same complaint.)

Practice statement'Evil and suffering prove God does not exist.'

Agree — material

Epicurus' inconsistent triad, used later by David Hume, says an omnipotent, omnibenevolent God and evil cannot all exist together. Atheists like Richard Dawkins point to natural evil — the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami killed around 230,000 people — which human free will cannot explain.

Disagree — material

Augustine's theodicy blames human free will and the Fall (Genesis 3), not God. Irenaeus and John Hick argue suffering is 'soul-making' that grows courage and compassion. Christians also say God shares our pain, because he suffers with humanity on the cross.

You could namethe inconsistent triadatheistsfree willmoral and natural evil
Listen for

Listen for how the speakers separate moral from natural evil, and note any answer they give for natural evil specifically — the hardest half of the question.

Lesson 1

The six beliefs of Islam

EpisodeThe Six Beliefs of Sunni Islam

Retrieval roulette — five questions, no notes
  1. Name the six beliefs of Islam.
  2. What does Iman mean?
  3. Which hadith collection records the six beliefs?
  4. Name the three categories Sunnis use for the six beliefs.
  5. Trap: is there original sin in Islam?
Score yourself out of five. Under four? This episode is tonight’s re-listen.
What this is about

Sunni Muslims share six key beliefs: Tawhid, angels, holy books, prophets, the Day of Judgement and Al-Qadr. Together they set out what a Muslim must accept to be a believer. They matter because they shape daily choices: if Allah is one, watching and judging, then worship and moral behaviour follow.

Key words — write your meaning in the Companion first, then check
Sunni
The largest group of Muslims, around 85%, who follow the six beliefs and the elected caliphs.
Iman
Faith; the beliefs a Muslim holds in the heart, says aloud and shows in actions.
Aqidah
A Muslim's set of core beliefs, learned as a creed that must be accepted.
Kutub
The holy books; the belief that Allah sent scriptures, ending with the Qur'an.
Shahadah
The declaration that there is no god but Allah and Muhammad is His messenger.
Source of wisdom

Qur'an 4:136 — believers are told to believe in Allah, His Messenger, the Book sent to him and the scriptures before it; whoever rejects Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers and the Last Day has gone far astray. (Paraphrase.)

Practice statement'The six beliefs of Islam are all equally important.'

Agree — material

Sunni Muslims take the six beliefs from the Hadith of Jibril, where Muhammad gives them in one single answer. Surah 4:136 warns that whoever rejects Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers or the Last Day has strayed far, so dropping any one belief breaks the whole faith.

Disagree — material

Many Sunni scholars argue Tawhid comes first, because every other belief depends on Allah being one. Surah 4:48 says shirk is the one sin Allah will not forgive, while a Muslim confused about angels is still counted as a believer. That looks like a ranking.

You could nameSunni articles of faithTawhidthe Shi'a five rootsQur'an 4:136
Listen for

Listen for whether the speakers treat the six beliefs as one package or keep pulling every belief back to Tawhid.

The five roots of Shi'a Islam

EpisodeThe Five Roots of Shi'a Islam

Retrieval roulette — five questions, no notes
  1. What are the five roots called?
  2. What does Adalat mean?
  3. Which root do Sunnis reject, and why?

What this is about

Shi'a Muslims organise their faith into five roots (Usul ad-Din): Tawhid, Adalat (divine justice), Nubuwwah (prophethood), Imamah (leadership by twelve appointed Imams) and Mi'ad (resurrection and judgement). These matter because they explain why Shi'a Muslims follow the Imams as infallible guides and insist Allah is always perfectly just.

Key words — write your meaning in the Companion first, then check
Usul ad-Din
The five roots of religion: Tawhid, Adalat, Nubuwwah, Imamate and Mi'ad (resurrection and judgement).
Shi'a

Jihad means 'to struggle' in Allah's cause. Greater jihad is the daily inner struggle to resist temptation and live faithfully. Key words — write your meaning in the Companion first, then check

Ummah
