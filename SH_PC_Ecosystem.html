Peace and Conflict

Eight topics, one summit. The divergent views set side by side, the key ideas connected, and the (d) evaluated the Level-4 way — everything for the top of the mark scheme, in one place.

The
big
question

Can religion bring peace to a world at war — and when, if ever, is a war just?

01

The divergent views explorer

Every (d) in this unit turns on the divergent views — set side by side, exactly as the examiner wants. Pick a topic, then tap a position to see what it claims. The Grade 9 move and the 7-minute discussion question travel with each topic.

Tap a circle — or use Tab and Enter — to explore each position.

The thread that ties it together

Grade 9 is not more content — it is three habits applied to every topic. Spot them and every (d) in this unit opens the same way.

Divergent views, side by side

Never one view at a time. Set them against each other — where they agree, and the exact point they part. That comparison IS the analysis.

The (d) regulation

Chain each point (claim → because → this means → therefore), steelman the other side, weigh BOTH arguments — a strength AND a limit — then justify the conclusion.

Textbook-first accuracy

A correctly named source beats a vague paraphrase. One learned line from each source, placed precisely, is worth more than a paragraph of drift.

Eight topics, one summit

Topic 1 · I25

Peace

Why do Muslims call Islam the House of Peace — and does that make Islam a religion of peace?
ASLAMAIslam comes from aslama — to surrender or submit to the will of God; submission comes first.
S-L-MThe same three consonants (s-l-m) spell salaam, peace — so submission IS peace, not two separate ideas.
INNERSubmitting to Allah brings peace inside a Muslim (Surah 6:127, the home of peace) — the aim of worship.
OUTERThat inner peace then flows out into peaceful, non-confrontational relationships and wider society.
Topic 2 · I27

Why wars occur

What causes wars, and how do Muslims and non-religious people respond to those causes?
THREE CAUSESThe chapter groups causes as religion, politics (nationalism/ethnicity) and resources — rarely just one.
THE LABELReligion is often the flag a war flies — Kashmir, Sudan and Syria look religious but hide other roots.
THE ROOTFitzgerald: religion is usually “an added factor”; the real roots are social or political, like land or power.
MANUFACTUREDGoering: leaders can “drag the people along” by calling peacemakers unpatriotic, making war feel unavoidable.
Topic 3 · I29

Just War

What makes a war just, and must a Muslim fight in one?
ALL THE CONDITIONSJust cause, proper authority, last resort, chance of success, proportional methods, civilians spared — six tests.
CUMULATIVEFail a single condition and the whole war is unjust — they chain together, so no war passes on cause alone.
WHY + HOWSome conditions test the reason for going to war, others how it is fought — pass both, or the war fails entirely.
THE MODERN TRAPA just cause still fails the test if modern weapons cannot spare civilians — some Muslims say none can today.
Topic 4 · I30

Holy War & jihad

What is a Holy War in Islam, and how does it relate to the greater and lesser jihad?
JIHADThe Arabic for “struggle” — the whole idea, not fighting on its own; most jihad involves no violence at all.
GREATERThe inner struggle to become a perfect Muslim — the greatest jihad, and it involves no fighting whatsoever.
LESSERThe outer struggle to remove evil from society — only this can involve force, and even then under strict rules.
HOLY WARHarb al-Muqadis is just one part of the lesser jihad, tightly limited by hadith that spare women and children.
Topic 5 · I28

Pacifism

What is pacifism, and can it sit alongside the teachings of Islam?
PACIFISMRefusing ALL violence, even turning the other cheek — the Qur’an has no such idea, so most Muslims reject it.
PASSIVE RESISTANCENon-violent OPPOSITION to authority — it still resists, just without weapons, as in the Arab Spring.
THE EVIDENCESurah 5:28 (Moses’ brothers withdraw); the Arab Spring toppled Mubarak non-violently in Tahrir Square.
KHANKhan Abdul Ghaffar Khan built a non-violent Muslim movement alongside Gandhi against British rule.
Topic 6 · I31

WMD

Can possessing weapons that could destroy millions ever be right?
6 AUG 1945The atomic bomb on Hiroshima killed 84,000 — yet Japan still would not surrender three days later.
9 AUG 1945A second bomb on Nagasaki killed 40,000 and finally brought the Second World War to an end.
MADSince both sides would be destroyed, no nuclear power dares strike — “no world war since 1945”, MAD claims.
THE FLAWDeterrence holds only while every leader stays rational; one failure risks worldwide nuclear extinction.
Topic 7 · I32

Victims of war

What does war cost its victims, and how do Muslim organisations work for peace?
THE COSTSCasualties, displacement (refugees) and expense — over 60 million died in WW2, most of them civilians.
RELIEFOne task is helping the victims — the refugees, the displaced and the injured left by every conflict.
PREVENTIONThe other is removing causes — the Ahmadiyya forum tackled the funding of terrorism as far back as 2014.
PROPHETIC JIHADThe Muslim Peace Fellowship’s non-violent struggle links straight back to the greater jihad’s ideal.
Topic 8 · I26

Forgiveness

Does lasting peace need justice, forgiveness, reconciliation — or all three?
JUSTICEFirst remove the cause: unjust laws breed rebellion, as in Syria against the Assad regime since 2011.
FORGIVENESSThen heal the resentment — God is merciful, so a Muslim must forgive others to be forgiven by Him.
RECONCILIATIONThen bring the enemies together — “make peace… between your contending brothers” (Surah 49:9–10).
LASTING PEACERepel evil with good until an enemy becomes “a friend and intimate” (Surah 41:34–35), ending hatred.

04

How to revise this unit

Use the resources in this order — the same loop as the lessons, so revision feels like coming home.

Bell work first — retrieve before you re-read

Five recall questions from memory, self-scored /5. Then the come-back tracker: the same five at +1 week and +1 month.

Listen along to the episode

One six-minute podcast per topic. Capture the six mind-map branches as it plays — the episode does the exposition so you can do the thinking.

Work the booklet pages

Four pages per topic: the key idea, two scripture quotes, the divergent views side by side, and the full (d). The Key Idea edition adds the 7-minute discussion.

Write the (d) the Level-4 way

Chain · steelman · weigh both (strength AND limit) · justify. Check yourself against the Level 3-vs-Level 4 models on lessons 3 and 6.

The marks map · (a) (b) (c) — 12 marks
(a) 3 marks — outline three. Three crisp sentences, no development needed.
(b) 4 marks — explain two. Each point + one development.
(c) 5 marks — explain two WITH a named source — one learned line, placed exactly.
The (d) · 12 marks — half the question
What it is — a full evaluation: agree and disagree developed, the non-religious voice engaged where the question invites it.
The grade 9 gap — Level 3 asserts its judgement; Level 4 chains, weighs both sides, and justifies a conclusion that survives the strongest counter.
